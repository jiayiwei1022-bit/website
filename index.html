Welcome!

I'm Jiayi, a PhD Candidate in Finance at INSEAD. I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

My research studies how new digital financial infrastructure — payment systems, data centers, and trading platforms — reshapes household welfare, local business dynamics, and the information embedded in market prices. To answer these questions, I build large-scale datasets that exist nowhere off the shelf, from a wallet-clustering and entity-labeling system that covers the full Bitcoin blockchain to ownership transaction records of facility-level AI infrastructure, and pair rigorous causal identification with economic models that turn local estimates into aggregate welfare answers.

My job market paper, The Real Effects of Anonymous Payment Systems, provides the first causal evidence on the social cost of digital payment anonymity. Using the staggered rollout of over 30,000 Bitcoin ATMs across U.S. counties as a laboratory, I show that access to anonymous payments raises local crime by 6.9 percent, fuels drug markets and overdose deaths, and erodes household balance sheets, with the heaviest burden on the most vulnerable communities. Using on-chain forensics to trace BTM-originated funds downstream and an informational model calibrated to those flows, I scale these local effects into a national measure of the cost of anonymous payment infrastructure.

Before INSEAD, I earned a Master's degree in Finance from Tsinghua University (PBCSF) and Bachelor's degrees in Economics from Peking University and in Linguistics from Beijing Language and Culture University.

Outside research, you might find me flipping through zines in a Paris art bookshop, or lingering where mountains meet rivers.
